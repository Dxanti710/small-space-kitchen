Five drawer problems in a small kitchen

A small kitchen does not fail because you picked the wrong color. It fails because one drawer is asked to hold bags, utensils, spices, lids, and “later.” Each problem is one object type. Check the live listing the day you buy. Prices move.

1. Crushed zip-top boxes

Sandwich-bag boxes collapse under anything heavier. A slotted in-drawer bag organizer is lanes for those boxes. Listing check: gallon / quart / snack boxes stand up in a standard drawer.

2. The utensil nest

Forks and the peeler become one pile. An expandable silverware tray stretches to the drawer width. Check depth and a well for serving pieces.

3. Spice jars you cannot read while the pan is hot

Round jars roll. An angled insert tilts labels up. Many need about 3 inches of drawer height.

4. Lid avalanche

One skillet, three lids on the floor. A vertical rack files lids and boards on edge.

5. The dark corner

Oil hides behind taller bottles. A 9–11 inch rimmed lazy Susan brings the back row around. The rim keeps glass from walking off.
